# Math Primary

A playful Grade 4 math learning game concept focused on making addition and subtraction practice feel like an adventure instead of a worksheet.

## Vision

Help children build speed, confidence, and number sense through short game loops, rewards, and friendly feedback.

## Game concept: **Number Quest**

The player explores a colorful map made of small math missions. Each mission mixes Grade 4 addition and subtraction with a simple goal, such as helping a character cross a bridge, unlock a treasure chest, or collect stars before time runs out.

### Target learner

- Grade 4 students
- Learners who benefit from visual reward systems
- Children who enjoy short, repeatable challenges

## Core learning focus

### Addition skills
- 2-digit and 3-digit addition
- Addition with regrouping/carrying
- Mental math using friendly numbers
- Word problem style addition prompts

### Subtraction skills
- 2-digit and 3-digit subtraction
- Subtraction with borrowing/regrouping
- Missing number equations
- Quick comparison and estimation tasks

## Core game loop

1. Player starts a mission.
2. A set of addition/subtraction challenges appears.
3. Correct answers earn coins, stars, and combo energy.
4. Streaks unlock power-ups or character items.
5. Mission ends with a short reward moment and progress update.

This keeps learning sessions short, satisfying, and easy to replay.

## Child engagement ideas

### 1. Adventure map progression
Children move through worlds such as:
- Jungle of Jumping Numbers
- Space Station Sum
- Treasure Island Take Away
- Castle of Clever Calculations

Each world can introduce slightly harder question patterns.

### 2. Instant feedback
- Correct answer: cheerful animation, sound, and positive phrase
- Incorrect answer: gentle correction and hint
- Second try support before showing full solution

### 3. Reward system
- Stars for accuracy
- Coins for completion
- Streak meter for consecutive correct answers
- Unlockable avatars, pets, badges, or trail effects

### 4. Choice and agency
Give the child small choices:
- Pick an avatar
- Choose a world theme
- Select "Practice", "Challenge", or "Boss Round"
- Use earned power-ups like "Freeze Timer" or "Hint Lens"

### 5. Short session design
Grade 4 learners usually respond well to quick wins. Aim for:
- 3 to 7 minute missions
- 5 to 10 questions per level
- One clear reward at the end of each mission

## Suggested mini-games

### Bridge Builder
Answer correctly to place planks and help a character cross a river.

### Treasure Chest
Solve equations to unlock chests and collect coins.

### Meteor Match
Pick the correct answer before a meteor reaches the planet.

### Bubble Pop
Pop the bubble that contains the correct answer from 3 or 4 options.

### Boss Battle
A friendly boss asks mixed addition and subtraction questions. A correct streak fills the hero power bar.

## Difficulty design for Grade 4

### Level 1: Warm-up
- 2-digit addition/subtraction
- Mostly direct equations
- Optional answer choices

### Level 2: Strategy
- 3-digit operations
- Regrouping and borrowing
- Fewer visual supports

### Level 3: Mixed challenge
- Missing numbers
- Timed rounds
- Short word problems

### Level 4: Mastery
- Mixed operations
- Multi-step tasks
- Greater focus on mental math efficiency

## Motivation without pressure
To keep the experience engaging and safe:
- Celebrate effort, not just speed
- Avoid harsh sounds or red failure screens
- Allow retry loops
- Show progress with bars, stickers, and world unlocks
- Include optional hints for struggling learners

## Simple UX recommendations
- Large buttons
- High contrast colors
- Minimal text on each screen
- Voice-friendly prompts where possible
- One task at a time on screen

## Parent/teacher value
A light dashboard can later show:
- Accuracy by skill
- Time spent
- Question types missed most often
- Improvement over recent sessions

## Recommended MVP
If starting small, build only this first:

### MVP loop
- Start screen
- Avatar selection
- One world
- Two mini-games
- Random addition/subtraction question generator
- Score, stars, and streak tracking
- End-of-level celebration screen

## Example question types for MVP
- `248 + 137 = ?`
- `532 - 198 = ?`
- `__ + 145 = 320`
- "A shop sold 126 apples in the morning and 149 in the afternoon. How many apples were sold altogether?"

## Product direction summary

Your idea is strong because gamification works especially well when:
- the learning goal is narrow,
- the session is short,
- the rewards are visible, and
- the child feels successful often.

For Grade 4 math, addition and subtraction are an excellent starting point because they support both fluency and confidence.
